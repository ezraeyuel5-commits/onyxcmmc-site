Who needs to consider Level 1?

CMMC Level 1 addresses basic protection of Federal Contract Information (FCI). FCI is nonpublic information provided by or generated for the government under a contract; public information and simple payment transactions are excluded from that definition. Read the FAR definition.

Confirm the requirement in the actual solicitation, contract or subcontract, including any applicable changes. A record of a DoD award or a company’s size is not enough to establish its CMMC level. If information handling includes CUI or a higher-level requirement, use the appropriate scope and support rather than this Level 1-only service.

The FAR safeguarding clause and the CMMC assessment/status requirement are related, but not interchangeable. Ask the contracting officer, prime or qualified counsel to resolve unclear contract language.

The annual assessment and affirmation cycle

Under 32 CFR 170.15 in the published final rule, Level 1 self-assessments are conducted annually and results are submitted in SPRS. Section 170.22 assigns the affirmation to the organization’s own senior-level representative with the required authority.

The assessment uses the mapped official assessment objectives, not just a checklist of document names. A Final Level 1 status requires the applicable security requirements to be met; Level 1 does not permit a plan of action and milestones as a route to a conditional status. An internal gap-tracking list is not a government-approved exception.

A practical sequence

  1. Confirm the contract and information scope. Establish what requirement applies and where FCI is processed, stored or transmitted. Use the official scoping guide rather than assuming every device is identical.
  2. Name the responsible people. Identify the business owner, technical owner and company affirming official. Agree who may access evidence and who may change systems.
  3. Review each safeguard and its objectives. Use the official assessment guidance. Our 15-item summary is an orientation aid, not the full objective set.
  4. Examine implementation and supporting evidence. Records, interviews and tests should match the actual environment. Do not turn a template or an unchecked screenshot into a MET result.
  5. Resolve and recheck gaps. Technical fixes stay with an authorized technical owner. Mark unsupported conclusions as unresolved.
  6. Arrange authorized SPRS access. Follow the current official SPRS access guidance. Do not share the affirming official’s credentials with a consultant.
  7. Submit and affirm accurate results. Review scope, CAGE information and the compliance result. Your senior official makes the affirmation under their own authorization.
  8. Hand over and retain the record. Retain assessment evidence for the rule’s six-year period, protect it appropriately, and track the next annual assessment/affirmation date.

What is recorded in SPRS?

Section 170.15 identifies the CMMC level, status date, assessment scope, associated CAGE code or codes, and compliance result as minimum results information. The affirmation separately identifies the official and their statement of continuing compliance. Use the current SPRS entry guide and tutorials for the interface, rather than an old screenshot.

Level 1’s result is not the numerical NIST SP 800-171 score often discussed for other assessments. Confirm exactly what a prime is requesting before sending a record.

Common questions

Do we need a third-party certification assessment?

Level 1 is self-assessment. Outside preparation help is optional; the company still owns its results and affirmation.

Can we affirm now and fix one failed item later?

Do not claim a Final Level 1 status with an unmet requirement. Level 1 does not permit a POA&M-based conditional status.

Must we buy a specific written policy pack?

No. DoD did not impose a universal Level 1 policy-template purchase requirement. You still need to implement the safeguards and retain the evidence used to support the assessment.

How long do we keep assessment evidence?

The Level 1 artifact-retention provision specifies six years from the CMMC Status Date. Choose appropriate protection and access controls for those records.

Does a small headcount prove Level 1 is the right level?

No. Headcount is an Onyx pricing criterion. The applicable contract requirements and information scope determine the compliance work.
