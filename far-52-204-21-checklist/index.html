What the clause covers

FAR 52.204-21 defines covered contractor information systems as contractor-owned or operated systems that process, store or transmit Federal Contract Information. FCI excludes information made public by the government and simple transactional information needed to process payments.

The clause also addresses qualifying subcontract flowdown, with an exclusion for commercially available off-the-shelf items. It does not eliminate other applicable safeguarding requirements. CMMC Level 1 assessment requirements must be considered alongside the actual contract, not inferred solely from the FAR clause.

How to use this checklist

  • Read the official clause and assessment objectives before assigning a result.
  • Identify the real implementation, its owner and the evidence you reviewed.
  • Treat the evidence examples below as illustrations—not universal document requirements or a complete checklist of proof.
  • Mark uncertainty honestly. A policy template is not a technical fix.
  • Do not upload contract documents, CUI, credentials or sensitive screenshots through a public inquiry form.

The CMMC final rule explains the Level 1 assessment approach and evidence retention. It does not require buying a particular consultant’s binder or policy pack.

The 15 safeguarding requirements

01 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(i)

Authorized access

Restrict system access to authorized people, processes acting for them, and devices.

Illustrative evidence: Approved-user and device records, account configuration, and checks of how access is granted or removed.

02 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ii)

Permitted functions

Limit users to the transactions and functions they are allowed to perform.

Illustrative evidence: Role and privilege settings, approvals and a check that permissions match assigned work.

03 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(iii)

External systems

Verify and control connections to, and use of, external information systems.

Illustrative evidence: An inventory of permitted external connections, technical restrictions and the process for exceptions.

04 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(iv)

Publicly accessible information

Control information posted or processed on publicly accessible systems.

Illustrative evidence: A review process and records showing that nonpublic contract information is not exposed publicly.

05 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(v)

Identification

Identify users, processes acting on their behalf, and devices.

Illustrative evidence: Account and device identifiers, plus evidence tying processes and accounts to authorized use.

06 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(vi)

Authentication

Verify those identities before allowing access to organizational systems.

Illustrative evidence: Authentication settings and a test of how access is validated. A screenshot alone may not resolve every objective.

07 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(vii)

Media disposal and reuse

Sanitize or destroy media containing FCI before disposal or release for reuse.

Illustrative evidence: A disposal/reuse process and relevant sanitization or destruction records.

08 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(viii)

Physical access

Limit physical access to systems, equipment and operating environments to authorized people.

Illustrative evidence: Access permissions, keys or badges, and checks of the actual physical arrangements.

09 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ix)

Visitors, logs and access devices

Escort and monitor visitors, retain physical-access audit logs, and manage physical-access devices.

Illustrative evidence: Visitor and access records, key/badge controls and a check of who follows the process.

10 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(x)

Communication boundaries

Monitor, control and protect communications at external and key internal system boundaries.

Illustrative evidence: A relevant network diagram, boundary-control configuration and evidence of how those controls operate.

11 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xi)

Public-facing components

Separate publicly accessible system components from internal networks using physical or logical subnetworks.

Illustrative evidence: A network design and configuration/test evidence showing the separation. A guest Wi-Fi name alone is not proof.

12 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xii)

Security flaws

Identify, report and correct system and information flaws promptly.

Illustrative evidence: An issue/patch process, reports, remediation records and checks of correction timing.

13 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xiii)

Malicious-code protection

Provide protection from malicious code at appropriate locations in the systems.

Illustrative evidence: Protection configuration and current coverage records for the relevant environment.

14 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xiv)

Protection updates

Update malicious-code protection when new releases become available.

Illustrative evidence: Update settings plus current version or update records—not only an unchecked policy.

15 · FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xv)

Periodic and real-time scanning

Run periodic system scans and real-time scans of external files when downloaded, opened or executed.

Illustrative evidence: Scan configuration, relevant test or operation records, and scheduled-scan results.

Evidence must reflect implementation

Use an appropriate mix of records, examination, interviews and tests for the mapped objectives. A single example in this guide is not an assurance that a requirement is met. The assessment must address the actual in-scope environment.

Level 1 does not permit a POA&M-based conditional status. Correct and recheck unmet requirements before making a Final Level 1 claim. See the self-assessment sequence and the affirmation responsibilities.

Common questions

Is this checklist the complete CMMC assessment?

No. It summarizes the 15 FAR safeguards. The official mapped assessment objectives and scope guidance determine what must be evaluated.

Why do some materials mention 17 rather than 15?

The FAR clause has 15 requirements. The final rule maps these to 17 NIST SP 800-171 requirements because one FAR requirement maps to three. Do not mix the numbering systems.

Does FAR 52.204-21 expressly name multi-factor authentication?

The clause requires identity authentication before access; it does not itself name MFA. Other requirements or a security design may require or recommend stronger methods.

Must every company have the same evidence documents?

No. Evidence should support the actual environment and assessment objectives. The examples here are not a mandatory universal artifact list.
